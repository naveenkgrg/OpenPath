# Internships and Mentorship Programs

These are structured, paid or stipended programs that provide mentorship, community, and real project experience. Most are designed for students and early-career contributors.

Most programs require at least some prior open-source contribution history before applying — so start contributing before deadlines open.

## Notes on each program

### Google Summer of Code
The most well-known student program. Competitive. You must apply to a specific project with a proposal. Most accepted students have already made at least one contribution to that project before the proposal. Check the GSoC site for the current year's participating organizations and timeline.

### Outreachy
Specifically for people underrepresented in tech. Participants are paid a stipend for 3 months of work on an open-source project. Applications require an initial contribution to the project during the application period — check the timeline and start early.

### LFX Mentorship
Run by the Linux Foundation. Hosts mentored projects across many ecosystems including CNCF, Hyperledger, and others. Applications are per-project per term. Three terms per year. More accessible than GSoC for first-timers.

### CNCF Mentored Projects
CNCF runs mentored projects specifically for cloud-native contributors through the LFX platform. Good entry point if you are interested in Kubernetes, Prometheus, Envoy, or other CNCF projects. Check the CNCF mentoring GitHub repo for current project listings.

## Tips for applying

- **Apply early to multiple programs.** Deadlines and acceptance rates vary. Do not depend on a single application.
- **Contribute before applying.** Most programs strongly prefer applicants who have already made at least one contribution to the relevant project. Start 1-3 months before deadlines.
- **Write a detailed proposal.** For GSoC and Outreachy especially, the quality of your project proposal is a significant factor. Review past accepted proposals that projects share publicly.
- **Engage with the community.** Join the project's Slack, mailing list, or Discord. Introduce yourself. Ask questions in public channels. Being a visible, positive community member before applying matters.
