# Contributor Success Stories

Real journeys from people who started contributing to open source and where it took them. These are not polished marketing copy — they are honest accounts of how people got started, what was hard, and what happened next.

---

## How to submit your story

Submit a pull request adding your story using the template below. Keep it honest and specific — generic advice is less useful than concrete details about what you actually did.

### Template

```markdown
## [Your Name or Handle] — [Short descriptor, e.g., "From zero to CNCF contributor in 6 months"]

**Background:** [One or two sentences: who you were when you started — student, career changer, self-taught, etc.]

**Where I started:** [What was your first contribution? What project? What issue?]

**What was hard:** [What took longer than expected? What did you get wrong the first time?]

**What helped:** [Resources, people, programs, or communities that made a difference.]

**What happened next:** [Where did it lead? Internship, job, community role, conference talk, mentorship program?]

**One piece of advice:** [What would you tell someone starting today?]
```

## Stories

### Naveen Garg — Maintainer of OpenPath

**Background:** Software engineer and community builder with experience in cloud infrastructure and open source. Started OpenPath to help others find their own path.

**Where I started:** Contributions to CNCF projects and documentation improvements across infrastructure tools. Early work was mostly documentation — fixing inaccuracies, improving clarity, adding missing steps.

**What was hard:** Finding the right project to start with. The ecosystem is large and it is easy to feel like every project is too complex. The turning point was narrowing down to one ecosystem (cloud-native) and starting with the smallest possible change.

**What helped:** LFX Mentorship structure, CNCF Slack community, and reading CONTRIBUTING.md files carefully before touching any code.

**What happened next:** Deeper involvement in the cloud-native community, leading to creating OpenPath as a resource for others who are where I was.

**One piece of advice:** Start smaller than you think you need to. A typo fix in a real project's docs is a real contribution. It gets your name in the commit history, it teaches you the workflow, and it builds the habit.
